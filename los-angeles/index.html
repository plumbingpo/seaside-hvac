Two common situations call for quick action from you before our technician gets there. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or gives off a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a much larger repair.

Keeping Your Los Angeles HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a system that runs for years and one that quits in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so small issues get caught early.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Los Angeles

Most local HVAC failures announce themselves before they happen. A breaker that trips more than once, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, a musty smell at startup. Those are early signs of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're a quick visit. Ignored, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
